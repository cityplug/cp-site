Find the cause first

Work out why the signal drops off.

Fast broadband doesn’t mean good Wi-Fi in every room. Where the router sits, the shape of the house, walls, floors, extensions and how far away your devices are all make a difference. We start with the rooms that are giving you trouble, then look at the router position, possible cable routes and where access points would work best.

Access points work best where they’re needed, not wherever’s easiest to fit them. If we can run a cable to an access point (called wired backhaul), it doesn’t have to pick up a weak signal and pass it on, so busy rooms and home offices get a more reliable connection.

Boosters and mesh systems have their place, but they’re not a guaranteed fix. A booster in a spot where the signal’s already poor has very little to boost. A mesh unit has the same problem if it can’t hold a good link back to the router. Your layout, and whether cables can be run, should decide which way to go.

Useful to tell us

  • Which rooms or areas are the problem
  • Whether it’s weak signal, drop-outs or slow speeds
  • Where your broadband router is
  • What you need to do there, like video calls, streaming or gaming

Homes and garden offices

Coverage for the whole house, not just the easy rooms.

We often hear about patchy Wi-Fi upstairs, rear extensions the router can’t reach, and garden offices where you need a connection you can rely on for work.

A garden office has to be planned as part of the route from the house. How far it is, the walls and outside space in between, power, and whether a cable can be run all affect what’s possible. We’ll talk you through the options, but the right one depends on your property and what can sensibly be installed.

  • Where the router is and what kit you have now
  • Anything you need for a garden office or outbuilding

Home Wi-Fi questions

Questions people usually ask

Will a faster broadband package fix weak Wi-Fi?

Not usually. Your broadband speed is what reaches the router. Wi-Fi coverage is how well that gets around your home. If one room has a weak signal, a faster package probably won’t change that.

Is mesh always better than a booster?

Not always. Both rely on being put in the right place and keeping a good link back to the router. Where we can run a cable, an access point wired back to the network avoids the wireless hop altogether.

Can you connect a garden office?

We’ll look at the options with you. The best approach depends on the distance, what’s in the way and whether a cable can be run between the house and the office.

How many access points will I need?

It depends on the layout, how the house is built, where you need coverage and what you use it for. Tell us the problem rooms and where the router is, and send a floor plan or a few photos, and we can give you a proper answer rather than a guess.
