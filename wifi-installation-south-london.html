Diagnose before adding hardware

Find out why the signal or speed falls away.

A fast broadband package does not automatically mean good Wi-Fi in every room. Router location, the shape of the property, walls, floors, extensions and the distance to the devices all affect coverage. CityPlug starts with the rooms and activities that are causing trouble, then considers the router position, likely cable routes and suitable access-point locations.

Access points work best when they are placed to serve the areas that need coverage, rather than hidden wherever installation is easiest. Where a cable route is practical, wired backhaul connects an access point to the network by cable. This avoids asking a distant wireless device to receive and retransmit the same connection, and can provide a more dependable foundation for busy rooms or fixed work areas.

Wireless boosters and mesh systems can be useful, but they are not automatic fixes. A booster placed where the original signal is already poor has little good signal to repeat. A wireless mesh node faces a similar limitation if it cannot maintain a sound link to the main router or another node. Property layout, placement and whether cabling is possible should guide the choice.

Homes and garden offices

Plan coverage for the whole property, not just the easiest room.

Typical enquiries include unreliable upstairs coverage, rear extensions beyond the router’s useful range, and garden offices where a stable connection matters for day-to-day work.

A garden office needs to be considered as part of the route from the main home network. Distance, walls, outside space, power and the possibility of a suitable cable route can all affect the available approach. CityPlug can discuss access-point and connectivity options, but the right solution depends on the property and what can be installed practically.

Home Wi-Fi questions

Frequently asked questions

Will a faster broadband package fix weak Wi-Fi?

Not necessarily. Broadband speed to the router and Wi-Fi coverage around the home are different parts of the connection. If the signal is weak in a particular room, changing the package alone may not address the location or coverage problem.

Is mesh always better than a booster?

No single option suits every property. Both depend on sensible placement and a good connection back to the main network. Where practical, an access point using wired backhaul avoids relying on a wireless hop between rooms.

Can you connect a garden office?

CityPlug can assess garden-office connectivity options. The practical approach depends on distance, obstructions and whether a suitable route is available between the house and office.

How many access points will I need?

That depends on the property layout, construction, coverage areas and intended use. Sharing the affected rooms, router position and a floor plan or photos helps CityPlug assess the job without guessing.
