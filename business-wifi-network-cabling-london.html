Business network installs

One practical network for staff, customers and essential business devices.

A business network often has to support several different jobs at once. Staff need access for day-to-day work, customers may expect guest Wi-Fi, and fixed equipment such as POS systems, card machines, printers and office devices may need wired connections. CityPlug plans the Wi-Fi and cabling together so each part of the installation has a clear purpose.

Wi-Fi access points, or APs, are positioned to provide wireless access where it is needed across the premises. Staff and guest networks can be kept separate, giving employees their own connection while visitors use a different network. Where a cable is the better fit for a fixed device, structured cabling creates organised routes back to the network rather than relying on loose or improvised connections.

The work can include cable labelling so it is easier to identify what serves each outlet or device. A straightforward handover then helps the person responsible for the site understand what has been installed, which connections serve key equipment and how the staff and guest networks are arranged.

Planning access

Fit the work around the premises.

Every site is different, so planning starts with what the business does, how the space is used and which areas and devices need a connection.

If the business is already open, share the trading hours and any periods when access is easier. This helps plan access to work areas, cable routes and the order of installation.

Before quoting, send

  • Business type, site size and postcode
  • Wi-Fi areas and approximate user/device numbers
  • POS, card machine, printer, office device or CCTV needs
  • Trading hours and preferred installation periods

Business Wi-Fi and cabling FAQs

Questions before you request a quote

Can staff and customers use different Wi-Fi networks?

Yes. A staff network can be kept separate from guest access, so employees and visitors do not have to use the same network. Include both requirements when describing the job.

What can be connected with structured cabling?

Typical business requirements include POS equipment, card machines, printers and office devices. The right layout depends on where the equipment is positioned and how the premises is used.

Can installation fit around opening times?

Share your trading hours and any access restrictions so suitable installation options can be discussed.

What happens at handover?

The handover is an opportunity to understand the installed access points, the staff and guest network arrangement, and the labelled connections serving fixed devices.

Useful details to include

Photos or a simple plan of the site can help show the layout, proposed equipment positions and possible cable routes. Also mention any access restrictions or areas that need to remain in use.
